---
id: K-EDNA
title: Interpreting environmental DNA (eDNA) read counts
source: ORCA background note (general eDNA methodology).
url: https://obis.org/manual/dnabarcoding/
---
Environmental DNA (eDNA) surveys detect organisms from DNA they shed into seawater. Metabarcoding assigns sequence reads to taxa, which makes eDNA good for presence detection and for finding rare or cryptic species.

Read counts are at best semi-quantitative. They depend on how much DNA each organism sheds, how fast it degrades, primer and PCR bias, sequencing depth and the reference database. Do not treat eDNA read counts as abundance or biomass, and compare reads across years only when sampling, primers and sequencing depth were held constant.

OBIS publishes DNA-derived occurrence records alongside conventional survey data, following the "Publishing DNA-derived data through biodiversity data platforms" guide.
