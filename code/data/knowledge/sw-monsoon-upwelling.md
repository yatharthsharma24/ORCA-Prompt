title: Southwest monsoon upwelling off the west coast of India
source: ORCA background note (textbook oceanography of the Arabian Sea; see INCOIS and ICAR-CMFRI publications for regional studies).
url: https://incois.gov.in
---
During the southwest monsoon (June to September) winds along the southwest coast of India drive coastal upwelling. Cooler, nutrient-rich subsurface water reaches the surface off Kerala and southern Karnataka, sea surface temperature drops, and phytoplankton blooms raise surface chlorophyll-a well above its pre-monsoon level. The bloom usually peaks in July and August and fades by October.

The strength of upwelling varies between years. Weak upwelling seasons often coincide with warm years in the Indian Ocean, such as El Nino and positive Indian Ocean Dipole years.

Small plankton-feeding pelagic fish, above all the Indian oil sardine, depend on this seasonal production. Fishery scientists commonly relate oil sardine recruitment and landings to upwelling intensity and chlorophyll in the same or the previous season.

In the northeastern Arabian Sea a separate winter bloom (December to March) is driven by convective mixing when cool, dry northeast-monsoon winds cool the surface.
