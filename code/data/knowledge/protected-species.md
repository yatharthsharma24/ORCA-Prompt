title: Olive ridley turtles and dugongs in Indian waters
source: ORCA background note (IUCN Red List categories; Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, Schedule I).
url: https://www.iucnredlist.org
---
The olive ridley turtle, Lepidochelys olivacea, is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. Odisha hosts some of the world's largest mass-nesting events (arribadas), at Gahirmatha, the Rushikulya river mouth and the Devi river mouth, usually between January and April. Seasonal fishing restrictions near these beaches aim to cut turtle bycatch in trawl and gill nets.

The dugong, Dugong dugon, is listed as Vulnerable globally. In India it survives in small, fragmented populations in the Gulf of Mannar and Palk Bay, the Gulf of Kutch, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Dugongs feed almost only on seagrass, so seagrass loss, bycatch and boat strikes are the main threats. Tamil Nadu notified a Dugong Conservation Reserve in Palk Bay in 2022.

Both species are protected under Schedule I of India's Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

Survey counts for rare marine mammals are small and vary with effort and weather, so year-to-year differences should be read with care. Trends need several years of comparable effort.
