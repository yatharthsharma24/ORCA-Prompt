title: Indian oil sardine (Sardinella longiceps) fishery
source: ICAR-Central Marine Fisheries Research Institute (CMFRI), annual marine fish landing estimates for India.
url: https://www.cmfri.org.in
---
The Indian oil sardine, Sardinella longiceps (family Dorosomatidae in the current WoRMS classification, traditionally placed in Clupeidae), is a short-lived, plankton-feeding coastal pelagic fish. It is one of the largest single-species fisheries in India, and most of the catch is landed in Kerala and Karnataka.

Oil sardine landings swing widely from year to year. ICAR-CMFRI landing estimates show high catches in the early 2010s followed by steep declines in the mid-2010s and again around 2019 to 2021. Proposed drivers include weak upwelling, warm sea surface temperature during El Nino years, and fishing pressure on juveniles.

Because the species lives only a few years, a poor spawning or recruitment season shows up in the catch quickly, often with a lag of about one year.
