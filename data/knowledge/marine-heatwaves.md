title: Defining marine heatwaves
source: Hobday, A.J. et al. (2016). A hierarchical approach to defining marine heatwaves. Progress in Oceanography 141, 227-238.
url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pocean.2015.12.014
---
Hobday et al. (2016) define a marine heatwave as a discrete, prolonged, anomalously warm water event: sea surface temperature above the seasonally varying 90th percentile of a baseline climatology (ideally 30 years) for at least five consecutive days. Gaps of two days or less between events are treated as one event.

Hobday et al. (2018, Oceanography 31(2)) added categories based on multiples of the difference between the 90th-percentile threshold and the climatological mean: moderate (1x), strong (2x), severe (3x) and extreme (4x).

With monthly data the five-day rule cannot be applied. A common approximation is to flag months whose value exceeds the 90th percentile of the same calendar month in the baseline period. The ORCA anomaly tool uses this monthly approximation and labels it as such.
